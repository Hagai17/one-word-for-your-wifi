# Candidates — One Word for Your Wi-Fi

Gate: a single word, 1–20 characters, written to `/output/entry.txt`.
Judge criteria, in order: (1) one word describing Wi-Fi as the entrant lives it;
(2) funny at once — a real laugh, not a pun that needs explaining; (3) true to how
home Wi-Fi actually behaves, recognisable; (4) avoids the words everyone reaches
for — a surprising angle on a familiar annoyance; (5) works as a single word with
no context, spelled so a reader gets it instantly.

## The field (12 candidates)

| # | Word | Chars | Angle |
|---|------|-------|-------|
| 1 | Buffering | 9 | The stock word. True, but it is the first word everyone reaches for. |
| 2 | Narcoleptic | 11 | Falls asleep without warning, mid-sentence, mid-call. |
| 3 | Gaslighting | 11 | "Connected • No internet" — the router insists everything is fine. |
| 4 | Ghosted | 7 | There one minute, gone the next, no explanation, no goodbye. |
| 5 | Peekaboo | 8 | Now you see it, now you don't — the signal plays peekaboo. |
| 6 | Goldilocks | 10 | Works in exactly one chair in the house; everywhere else is wrong. |
| 7 | Houdini | 7 | Vanishing act as a lifestyle. |
| 8 | Asthmatic | 9 | Wheezes and struggles for breath the moment the whole family streams. |
| 9 | Teenager | 8 | Moody, sleeps in, ignores you, works when it feels like it. |
| 10 | Flickering | 10 | Comes and goes like a dying light bulb. |
| 11 | Siesta | 6 | Takes little naps at random hours. |
| 12 | Part-time | 9 | A connection with part-time hours and no notice. |

## Duels (criteria applied in order; finals played in both orders)

**Buffering vs Flickering** — Both on-subject and true. Buffering is the more
instant laugh, but criterion 4 is decisive the other way: Buffering is the single
most reached-for word in this field and will arrive in hundreds of copies.
Flickering survives the round; Buffering is cut. *Winner: Flickering.*

**Siesta vs Narcoleptic** — Same joke family (the connection sleeps), two
strengths. Siesta is a gentle nap you can plan around; Narcoleptic is the sudden,
involuntary, mid-Zoom-call collapse — the behaviour everyone actually hates, so
it wins criterion 3 (truth) outright, and the clinical word landing on a home
router is the bigger laugh (criterion 2). *Winner: Narcoleptic.*

**Teenager vs Asthmatic** — Teenager is a person, and its truth is vague
(moodiness in general); Asthmatic names a specific failure — the connection
audibly struggling under load — so it wins on criteria 1 and 3. Teenager cut.
*Winner: Asthmatic.*

**Part-time vs Ghosted** — Part-time needs its hyphen and a half-beat of thought;
Ghosted is one clean word, instantly spelled, and names the exact experience of
a connection that vanishes without a word. Ghosted wins criteria 2 and 5.
*Winner: Ghosted.*

**Houdini vs Peekaboo** — Both play disappearance. Houdini is a proper noun and
implies a performer who *chooses* to vanish; Peekaboo is the behaviour itself —
the signal popping in and out, teasing you — which is truer to a fluctuating
connection (criterion 3) and lands faster (criterion 2). *Winner: Peekaboo.*

**Gaslighting vs Goldilocks** — The two strongest "specific annoyance" angles.
Gaslighting nails the single most infuriating screen in home networking
("Connected, no internet"); Goldilocks nails the one-magic-spot geography of
every house. On criterion 2 Gaslighting gets the bigger, angrier laugh of
recognition; on criterion 4, though, Gaslighting is fashionable slang that many
entrants will also reach for, while Goldilocks is a genuinely surprising pick.
Gaslighting takes the duel on laugh size. *Winner: Gaslighting.*

### Semifinals

**Flickering vs Narcoleptic** — Flickering describes a weak, wavering signal;
Narcoleptic describes the connection dying outright, asleep at the worst moment.
Narcoleptic is funnier (2), truer to the most hated behaviour (3) and fresher
(4). *Winner: Narcoleptic.*

**Asthmatic vs Peekaboo** — Asthmatic's metaphor takes a half-beat to unpack;
Peekaboo is understood before you finish reading it and is the more playful
laugh. Peekaboo wins criterion 2 by enough to settle it. *Winner: Peekaboo.*

**Ghosted vs Gaslighting** — Ghosted is shorter and cleaner (criterion 5), but
Gaslighting is the more specific accusation: the Wi-Fi doesn't just leave, it
*lies to your face about being connected*. Bigger laugh (2), sharper truth (3).
*Winner: Gaslighting.*

### Final — three-way, each pair in both orders

**Narcoleptic vs Peekaboo**
- Order A (Narcoleptic first): C1 tie. C2 — Narcoleptic's image of the
  connection falling asleep mid-sentence is a knowing, adult laugh; Peekaboo is
  a giggle at a baby's game, cute before it is funny. C3 — the sudden total
  dropout is the most recognisable Wi-Fi pain there is; slight edge Narcoleptic.
- Order B (Peekaboo first): same reading reversed — Peekaboo's edge is
  playfulness only, while Narcoleptic wins truth by more than Peekaboo wins
  anywhere. Consistent winner: **Narcoleptic**.

**Narcoleptic vs Gaslighting**
- Order A: C2 tie-ish (both land instantly). C3 — "falls asleep without warning"
  describes the behaviour of the *connection itself*; Gaslighting describes one
  (admittedly beloved) error message. C4 — Gaslighting is trendy slang many
  entrants will submit; Narcoleptic is the surprising angle. Narcoleptic.
- Order B: Gaslighting's best criterion is 2, and it does not beat Narcoleptic
  there by much; Narcoleptic's wins on 3 and 4 are larger. Consistent winner:
  **Narcoleptic**.

**Peekaboo vs Gaslighting** (for second place) — Gaslighting, on laugh size and
specificity of the annoyance.

## Winner

**Narcoleptic** — 11 characters.

It is the diagnosis every home Wi-Fi deserves: a connection that falls asleep
without warning, in the middle of the thing you needed it for, and wakes up
later acting like nothing happened. It wins on the judge's own ordering — an
instant laugh, the truest description of the most familiar annoyance, an angle
almost nobody else will take, and a single instantly-spelled word that also
happens to be a perfect network name.
